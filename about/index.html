Pregnancy apps tell you what to expect. They don’t tell you where you stand.

At 14 weeks you’re still feeling sick, and you wonder if that’s normal. Week-by-week articles say sickness “usually fades in the second trimester”, but not how many people are still in the same boat today.

Bump & Compare turns your daily check-ins into a clear answer: about 4 in 10 people still feel sick at 14 weeks, and for most it eases over the next few weeks. When something isn’t normal, it tells you who to call.

How it works

Thirty seconds a day. Answers that make sense.

Daily check-in

One question per screen, in plain words. “Feeling sick” and “vomiting” are separate, and every answer explains what it means.

Compare at your week

See how many people out of 10 have each symptom at your exact stage, and whether it usually eases or rises from here.

Warning signs, clearly

Tick a warning sign and the app tells you exactly who to contact: your midwife, maternity unit, NHS 111 or 999.

Notes for your midwife

A plain summary of your last 14 days, ready to copy or read out, so nothing gets forgotten at your appointment.

Coming up

What usually happens over the next four weeks, plus milestones like first movements and your 20-week scan.

Your patterns

After 14 check-ins, see what tends to go with your symptoms, like smells, skipped meals or a bad night’s sleep.

Linked symptoms

Research-backed tips when symptoms go together. For example, treating heartburn may also ease sickness.

Myth or fact?

A weekly old wives’ tale to guess, then the answer and the evidence. Does a fast heartbeat mean a girl? (No.)

The science

Every number comes from published research, with an honest evidence rating.

Where a study only measured some weeks, the weeks in between are smoothed, and the app says so. A midwife will review every figure before launch.

Symptom What the studies found Evidence Source
Feeling sick74% had nausea. Half had stopped by 14 weeks, 9 in 10 by 22 weeksGoodLacroix et al. 2000; Gadsby et al. 1993
Vomiting52% vomit at some point. Weekly shape estimated from nauseaSomeGadsby et al. 1993
Heartburn22%, 39% and 72% in the first, second and third trimesterGoodMarrero et al. 1992
Back pain56% at 24 weeks, 62% at 30, 58% at 36. Earlier weeks estimatedSomeThorell & Kristiansson 2012
Trouble sleepingAbout half in the first trimester, fewer in the second, about 7 in 10 near full termSomeZhang et al. 2021; Fan et al. 2025
TirednessAround 9 in 10 at some point. No reliable weekly figures yetLimitedEffati-Daryani et al. 2021

Later on, people will be able to share their check-ins anonymously if they choose. Community figures will only appear for a week once at least 200 people have logged it, and always beside the research figures, never instead of them.

Made for you if…

You want reassurance

First pregnancy, still feeling sick, and you just want to know if it’s normal for your week.

You like to be prepared

You see your midwife every few weeks and want a clear summary instead of trying to remember everything.

Something feels off

Severe sickness or an unusual symptom, and you need fast, calm signposting to the right care.

Privacy and safety

Your pregnancy is your business.

Pregnancy data is health data, so privacy comes first, not as an add-on.

  • Private by defaultYour check-ins stay on your phone. Sharing anything is your choice.
  • No ads, no trackersWe never sell data or put advertising trackers on health information.
  • Midwife-reviewedWarning-sign wording and every figure will be checked by a midwife before launch.
  • Not a diagnosisIt shows typical patterns and points you to care. It never replaces your midwife.
